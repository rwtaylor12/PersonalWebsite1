Build log

How this site was built

Every change to this site starts with a prompt. Here's the trail.

v1 — The first prompt

The very first thing I typed into Claude Code:

I am wanting to build a personal website, a few different tabs/landing pages to show off my personality, projects, CV/resume, and show my (currently limited) ability to prompt/use/code with AI.

What came back: a four-page static site (About, Projects, Resume, AI Lab) using plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, plus a dark mode and a printable resume.

What I learned: a short prompt works, but the AI has to guess a lot (my name, my style, my projects). My next prompts give it much more context.

v2 — Make it mine

The AI gave me a fill-in-the-blanks template. I filled in the parts I was ready to share and told it to skip the rest:

One-line intro: the road goes on forever, the party never ends --include hyperlink to the Robert Earl Keen Song on YouTube.
Short bio: I'm Ryan --> Business Faculty @ SAU Tech / Dad to Owen (4) & Reid (1) / Husband to Haley (Age N/A) / ...
Jobs: (lets just upload the resume later and skip this)
Colour/style preference: Blues & grays, modern/sleek style.

What came back: my real About page, a cooler blue-and-gray theme with sleeker headings, and my name across the site.

What I learned: I paused the AI halfway through ("I need to keep working on that. Pause.") and it stopped cleanly. Pausing, finishing my own notes and then sending everything at once gave a better result than rushing.

Rules of thumb

What I've picked up so far

1. Context beats cleverness

Most bad answers come from missing information, not from bad wording.

2. Ask for a plan first

Reviewing a plan is faster than untangling code I didn't ask for.

3. Check its work

AI is confident even when it's wrong. I verify facts and test anything it builds.

4. Iterate in small steps

One change per prompt makes it easy to see what worked and roll back what didn't.
